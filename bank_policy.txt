GLOBAL MERCHANTS BANK - INSTITUTIONAL UNDERWRITING RISK POLICY
Document ID: URP-2026-V2.1
Policy Version: 2.1
Effective Date: May 11, 2026
Policy Owner: Credit Risk Management Division
Classification: Internal Use

SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This document establishes the mandatory underwriting risk thresholds,
assessment criteria, and automated decision parameters applicable to
consumer lending products issued by Global Merchants Bank.

The purpose of this policy is to maintain consistent credit assessment
standards, control exposure to default risk, and ensure that automated
underwriting decisions are made in accordance with the Bank's approved
credit risk framework.

All automated underwriting decisions must operate within the
parameters defined by this policy. Applications falling outside
validated underwriting parameters must be subject to the applicable
manual review or escalation procedures.

SECTION 2: DEMOGRAPHIC CRITERIA

Section 2.1: Minimum and Maximum Age Restrictions

The applicant must possess the legal capacity to enter into a binding
financial contract. Accordingly, the minimum eligible applicant age
for any consumer credit facility is established at 18 years.

Applications submitted for applicants below 18 years of age shall not
be eligible for automated approval.

For data validation and underwriting purposes, applications containing
a stated applicant age exceeding 100 years shall be treated as
invalid and automatically declined by the underwriting system unless
referred for appropriate data verification or manual review.

SECTION 3: FINANCIAL CAPACITY AND LEVERAGE

Section 3.1: Annual Income Assessment

The applicant must demonstrate sufficient and reasonably verifiable
income to support the requested debt obligation.

The stated annual income (person_income) is a primary indicator of
repayment capacity and must be evaluated in conjunction with the
requested principal, employment history, loan pricing, and other
available risk characteristics.

Lower-income applicants are not automatically excluded from credit.
However, applications involving lower verified income require
appropriate consideration of the requested principal and resulting
leverage exposure.

Section 3.2: Principal Loan Amount Limitations

The principal loan amount (loan_amnt) requested by a retail consumer
must be assessed against the applicant's demonstrated financial
capacity.

Higher principal amounts represent increased credit exposure to the
Bank and therefore require stronger supporting characteristics across
income, employment stability, and leverage measures.

Requests at the upper limits of the Bank's approved lending range may
be subject to enhanced underwriting scrutiny and additional
verification requirements.

Section 3.3: Income-to-Loan Ratio (Leverage Restrictions)

The primary leverage metric used within the automated underwriting
framework is Loan Percent of Income (loan_percent_income), calculated
as the total requested loan amount divided by the applicant's verified
gross annual income.

Applications presenting a loan_percent_income value greater than
0.35 (35%) shall be subject to heightened underwriting scrutiny due to
the increased repayment burden relative to stated income.

A loan_percent_income value equal to or greater than 0.50 (50%)
represents an unacceptable level of leverage under this policy and
shall result in automatic rejection, irrespective of other
compensating applicant characteristics or existing banking
relationships.

SECTION 4: EMPLOYMENT STABILITY

Section 4.1: Minimum Employment History

Continuous employment history is considered an important indicator of
income stability and repayment capacity.

Applicants with a stated employment history (person_emp_length) of
less than 2.0 years shall be classified as presenting an elevated
employment-stability risk.

Such applications require stronger compensating characteristics in
other areas of the credit assessment before approval may be considered.

Section 4.2: Maximum Validated Employment Limits

Employment histories exceeding 60 years shall be treated as
potentially invalid application data.

Such values are inconsistent with normal employment-history ranges and
may indicate data-entry errors or inaccurate applicant information.
Applications containing such values shall be placed on hold for data
validation or manual review rather than being processed as standard
underwriting observations.

SECTION 5: RISK-BASED PRICING

Section 5.1: Interest Rate Risk Adjustments

The sanctioned loan interest rate (loan_int_rate) forms part of the
applicant's overall risk profile and may vary according to the
composite credit risk assessment.

Applicants presenting elevated risk characteristics, including limited
employment history, lower verified income, or elevated
income-to-loan ratios, may be assigned higher risk-based pricing in
accordance with the Bank's approved pricing framework.

Applications assigned an interest rate exceeding 18.0% shall be
classified as a high-risk pricing tier and require secondary
underwriter validation before final approval.

SECTION 6: AUTOMATED UNDERWRITING, COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

The Bank's automated underwriting system uses approved machine
learning models to evaluate applicant risk and produce an initial
credit classification.

The automated XGBoost underwriting model is required to operate within
the hard eligibility and risk parameters established by this policy.

Model predictions must not be interpreted as a replacement for the
Bank's approved underwriting policy. Where a mandatory policy
threshold requires rejection, the application must not be approved
solely on the basis of a favorable model prediction.

Any material deviation from the underwriting parameters defined in
this policy must be documented and handled through the Bank's
authorized review and escalation procedures.

Override capabilities are restricted to authorized personnel and are
subject to appropriate monitoring and audit controls.

SECTION 7: DECISION EXPLANATION AND AUDITABILITY

Where an application is rejected, the Bank must maintain an
appropriate record of the principal factors contributing to the
adverse credit decision and provide an explanation to the applicant
where required by applicable law, regulation, or Bank procedure.

For automated decisions, model explanation methods may be used by
authorized underwriting and audit personnel to identify the relative
contribution of individual applicant characteristics to the model's
prediction.

Policy-based explanations provided to applicants must be grounded in
the applicable provisions of this Underwriting Risk Policy and must
not attribute a decision to a policy requirement that is not stated
within the policy.

SECTION 8: POLICY GOVERNANCE

This policy is owned by the Credit Risk Management Division and is
subject to periodic review.

Changes to underwriting thresholds, automated decision rules, or
model enforcement parameters require appropriate authorization through
the Bank's established credit risk and model governance procedures.
